About the model. This is a simple SIR (susceptible → infected → recovered) model. Each infected ball stays contagious for a fixed time, and the chance of passing it on per bounce is tuned so one case causes about R₀ new cases in a fully susceptible crowd. The vaccine is "all or nothing": each vaccinated person is either fully protected (with probability = efficacy) or not protected at all. The charts use the standard final-size equation for the same model, which assumes a large, well-mixed population. With only ~450 balls the animation is noisier: small outbreaks sometimes die out by luck, which also happens in real life.

Vaccine risk. Each vaccinated person dies from the vaccine with the chosen probability, before the outbreak starts. The measles presets use 1 death per 10 million doses and the other presets use 1 per million. These are deliberately pessimistic round numbers: for most routine vaccines, deaths actually caused by the vaccine are rarer than that. The "risky vaccine" lesson is hypothetical, far riskier than any approved vaccine. With only ~450 balls, risks this small almost never show up in the animation; the charts show their effect for a full population.

Real measles vaccine (MMR) risks, approximate. In healthy people, no death risk has been established; large safety reviews (including the US Institute of Medicine in 2004 and 2011) found none. The one clearly vaccine-caused fatal problem is a brain infection from the vaccine strain in people with severely weakened immune systems, who are not supposed to receive this live vaccine. Deaths reported to VAERS (the US vaccine adverse-event reporting system) are unverified reports, not proven cases. Serious but usually non-fatal side effects (CDC): febrile seizures in about 1 in 3,000–4,000 doses (frightening, but no lasting harm); low platelet count (ITP) in about 1 in 40,000 doses, usually clearing up by itself; severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) in about 1.8–14.4 per million doses, treatable and rarely fatal. The vaccine-risk slider counts deaths only, so it does not model these side effects. More detail on the MMR side effects and MMR and autism pages.

Measles itself, for comparison (CDC). About 1–3 deaths per 1,000 children infected in the US (far more where malnutrition is common or care is hard to reach); brain inflammation (encephalitis) in about 1 per 1,000 cases; and SSPE, a fatal brain disease that appears years later, in about 4–11 per 100,000 cases overall, rising to roughly 1 in 600–1,400 when measles is caught in infancy or early childhood.

Preset numbers are rough, commonly cited estimates chosen to show the ideas. They are not forecasts, and real fatality rates vary a lot with age, health care and other factors. This is a teaching tool, not medical advice.
